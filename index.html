We created ScamSafe since we saw that a growing issue existed—the faster technology develops, the more people, particularly older individuals, become susceptible to sophisticated cyber scams. We decided to take action on it.

That's how we came up with ScamSafe, an interactive educational platform with easy-to-understand videos and quizzes. However, we didn't want it to seem like another boring educational site. // AI Academy / Module 01

AI, in plain language

Generative AI creates likely next pieces of text, images, audio, or code from patterns learned during training. It does not automatically know whether an answer is true.

What is happening?

A language model receives your prompt and predicts a sequence of tokens that fits the patterns it learned. It can be useful at summarizing, drafting, translating, and brainstorming.

That process is not the same as checking a live source or reasoning like a person. A model can make up a citation, miss recent events, reflect bias in its data, or misunderstand your request.

Remember: fluent wording is not evidence. Ask for sources, then open and verify the sources yourself.

Tips

  • Sit next to people, not in front of them. Let them hold the device.

Help & Glossary

Think you got scammed? Start here.

  1. Stop talking to them. Don't call back.
  2. Call your bank or card company and tell them.
  3. Change your passwords, email first.
  4. Write down names, numbers, dates and amounts.
  5. Report it (links below) and tell someone you trust. It happens to smart people every day.
Report to the FTC FBI IC3 Identity theft help Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311

Double check these are still current before you rely on them. If you're in danger right now, call 911.

Protect a phone or computer

ScamSafe is a learning website, not a firewall. A webpage cannot inspect or block attacks across your phone, remove malware, or guarantee that a device is safe. Use the built-in protections from your device maker and mobile carrier.

  • Install operating-system and app updates from Settings or the official app store. Do not install an update from a pop-up, text, or caller.
  • Use a screen lock, a unique email password, and multi-factor authentication. Never approve a sign-in prompt you did not start or share its code.
  • Remove apps you do not recognize. Review device administrator, accessibility, VPN, and configuration-profile settings for access you did not grant.
  • Back up important files regularly. Install apps only from the official store and review requested permissions.

If you think a device is compromised

  1. Stop using it for banking, shopping, and password changes. Do not follow instructions from the caller or pop-up.
  2. From a different trusted device, secure your email first, change reused passwords, and sign out other sessions where available.
  3. Call your carrier from a trusted phone if service suddenly stops or you suspect a SIM swap. Contact your bank if payment or account details may be exposed.
  4. Ask the device maker or a trusted repair professional for help. Avoid unknown “cleaner” apps and do not factory-reset before considering safe backups and evidence.

If there is immediate danger, use another phone to contact emergency services.
