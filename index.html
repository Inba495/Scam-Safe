    // Cloudverse / Beta lesson 01

    Cloud basics

    Sync is not the same as backup

    Cloud sync keeps a service's copy aligned with your devices. If a file is deleted or overwritten, that change may sync too. A separate backup or version history can help recover earlier copies.

    Use a unique password and multifactor authentication. Never follow a surprise “storage full” sign-in link; open your provider's official app or type its address yourself.

    Quick check

    Your synced folder suddenly loses a file on every device. What should you check first?

    // Cloudverse / Beta lesson 02

    Protect a cloud account

    Make account takeovers harder

    • Use a unique password stored in a password manager.
    • Enable multifactor authentication or a passkey where supported.
    • Review signed-in devices and revoke ones you no longer recognize.
    • Never share a one-time sign-in or recovery code with anyone.

    Spot a fake cloud alert

    A text says your files will be deleted unless you “verify billing” through a shortened link. Don't tap it. Open the provider's official app, check account notices there, and contact support using its official site.

    // Cloudverse / Beta lesson 03

    iCloud & Apple Account safety

    Review your own account safely

    On your Apple devices, review the devices signed in to your Apple Account, keep trusted phone numbers current, and turn on two-factor authentication. Menu labels may change across software versions.

    Never give a caller your Apple Account password or verification code. ScamSafe does not connect to iCloud and will never ask you to sign in here.

    Safer recovery: set recovery options while you are signed in, keep your device passcode private, and use Apple's official support site if you are locked out.

    // Cloudverse / Beta lesson 04

    Share files with care

    Choose the smallest access needed

    Before sharing, check the recipients, link access, and whether they can view or edit. Avoid public “anyone with the link” access for private files. Remove old collaborators when a project ends, and stop sharing if a link was sent to the wrong person.

    Practice decision

    A folder contains private class records. A teammate only needs one worksheet.

    Share only that worksheet with the teammate's account and view-only access if that is enough—not the whole folder with a public link.

    // Cloudverse / Beta lesson 05

    Backups & account recovery

    Make a recovery plan before you need one

    1. Keep recovery email and trusted numbers current.
    2. Save recovery codes offline in a private, secure place.
    3. Back up important files to a separate destination and test restoring one.
    4. If a device is lost, use the provider's official lost-device tools and change credentials from a trusted device.

    No cloud credentials are collected by these lessons. The examples are educational and may not match the latest provider menus.

    // AI Academy / Module 01

    AI, in plain language

    Generative AI creates likely next pieces of text, images, audio, or code from patterns learned during training. It does not automatically know whether an answer is true.

    What is happening?

    A language model receives your prompt and predicts a sequence of tokens that fits the patterns it learned. It can be useful at summarizing, drafting, translating, and brainstorming.

    That process is not the same as checking a live source or reasoning like a person. A model can make up a citation, miss recent events, reflect bias in its data, or misunderstand your request.

    Remember: fluent wording is not evidence. Ask for sources, then open and verify the sources yourself.

    // AI Academy / Module 05

    AI media & deepfakes

    Generated or edited media can look and sound convincing. A clip is a reason to verify a claim, not proof that the claim is real.

    Three checks before you share

    1. Find the earliest available version and check who published it.
    2. Look for independent reporting or an official statement.
    3. Do not rely on visual glitches alone; real media can look strange and fakes can look polished.

    If a clip requests money, codes, or urgent action, contact the person using a number or channel you already trust.

    // AI Academy / Module 06

    Privacy & fairness

    AI tools may retain prompts, reflect gaps in their training data, or make mistakes about people. Share less, question outputs, and keep consequential decisions accountable to people.

    A safer way to experiment

    • Use fictional examples instead of real names, account details, or private records.
    • Check your school or workplace rules before using an AI tool for its information.
    • Ask who could be harmed if an AI output is wrong or unfair.

    // AI Academy / Module 07

    Choose the right AI task

    AI is most useful when the task is low-risk, the inputs are safe to share, and a person can check the result. Practice choosing the right tool for the job.

    A quick decision filter

    1. Could a wrong answer harm someone or affect a major decision?
    2. Would using AI expose private information or break a rule?
    3. Can you check the result before using it?

    If the risk is high, use a qualified person and trusted sources—not automation alone.

    // AI Academy / Module 08

    Improve an AI answer

    Good AI use is a loop: ask clearly, inspect the result, verify important details, and revise. A polished answer is still only a draft.

    The answer-check routine

    • Specific: Does it answer the actual question?
    • Supported: Can you verify facts, names, numbers, and sources?
    • Safe: Is private information protected, and could the result unfairly affect someone?
    • Useful: Is the format clear for the person who needs it?

    // AI Academy / Module 09

    Build safer AI-assisted code

    AI can speed up a first draft, but generated code is untrusted input. Review it as carefully as code from an unfamiliar contributor, and test it in a safe environment before using it.

    A secure review checklist

    • Protect secrets: Never paste API keys, passwords, private customer data, or production logs into an AI tool. Keep secrets in a server-side secret store, not browser code or a public repository.
    • Validate inputs: Treat user input and AI-generated output as untrusted. Check expected types, lengths, and allowed values before using them.
    • Use safe data access: Prefer parameterized database queries and established libraries over building queries from strings.
    • Limit access: Give each account only the data and actions it needs. Enforce permissions on the server and in database policies, not only by hiding buttons in the page.
    • Check dependencies: Review package names, maintainers, versions, and known advisories before installing generated suggestions.
    • Test and review: Add tests for expected and invalid inputs, inspect the actual changes, and use a second human review for sensitive features.
    Remember: passing tests is useful evidence, not a guarantee that software cannot be hacked.

    Help & Glossary

    Think you got scammed? Start here.

    1. Stop talking to them. Don't call back.
    2. Call your bank or card company and tell them.
    3. Change your passwords, email first.
    4. Write down names, numbers, dates and amounts.
    5. Report it (links below) and tell someone you trust. It happens to smart people every day.
    Report to the FTC FBI IC3 Identity theft help Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311

    Double check these are still current before you rely on them. If you're in danger right now, call 911.

    Protect a phone or computer

    ScamSafe is a learning website, not a firewall. A webpage cannot inspect or block attacks across your phone, remove malware, or guarantee that a device is safe. Use the built-in protections from your device maker and mobile carrier.

    • Install operating-system and app updates from Settings or the official app store. Do not install an update from a pop-up, text, or caller.
    • Use a screen lock, a unique email password, and multi-factor authentication. Never approve a sign-in prompt you did not start or share its code.
    • Remove apps you do not recognize. Review device administrator, accessibility, VPN, and configuration-profile settings for access you did not grant.
    • Back up important files regularly. Install apps only from the official store and review requested permissions.

    If you think a device is compromised

    1. Stop using it for banking, shopping, and password changes. Do not follow instructions from the caller or pop-up.
    2. From a different trusted device, secure your email first, change reused passwords, and sign out other sessions where available.
    3. Call your carrier from a trusted phone if service suddenly stops or you suspect a SIM swap. Contact your bank if payment or account details may be exposed.
    4. Ask the device maker or a trusted repair professional for help. Avoid unknown “cleaner” apps and do not factory-reset before considering safe backups and evidence.

    If there is immediate danger, use another phone to contact emergency services.

      ScamSafe Privacy Policy

      Your data is not for sale

      No selling: ScamSafe does not sell, rent, or trade your personal information or email address to marketing companies or third parties. Our purpose is to help people learn to recognize scams, not to profit from their data.

      1. Your choices and control

      You may edit optional profile details in your account. Signed-in accounts are discoverable by username in the community directory, and Gmail addresses are also visible and searchable by signed-in members; other email providers are not shown. You can block a member. Blocking hides the connection and messages in the app but does not erase stored records. This version does not provide an in-app account deletion button. To request deletion of a cloud account, contact the ScamSafe administrator through the support contact provided to you. Account records, learning progress, messages, and profile photos may require removal in Supabase. Browser-stored information can be removed by clearing this site's data in your browser; doing so does not delete cloud data.

      2. AI lessons and information you enter

      The AI Academy's lesson practice tools run in your browser. The chatbot uses an OpenAI API key that you provide. The key, your question, and recent chat context are sent directly from your browser to OpenAI over HTTPS; ScamSafe servers do not receive or store the key or conversation. The key and chat exist in page memory only and are cleared when you reload or clear the chat. OpenAI may process requests under its own privacy and retention terms. While the key is active, it is accessible to this page; a compromised page or malicious browser extension could expose it. Use a key you control, set provider-side spending limits, and revoke it when finished. Do not include passwords, sign-in codes, financial account details, private records, or another person's sensitive information.

      3. What information is used

      • Account: Supabase Auth handles your email address and sign-in credentials. Your username is stored in the account profile.
      • Optional profile information: You may provide a phone number, display name, short bio, age range, gender choice, and profile photo. Profile photos are for display only; ScamSafe does not analyze them to identify, recognize, or verify anyone. You can replace or remove your photo from your account. The signup form uses your date of birth only in your browser to calculate an age range; it does not send or store the exact birth date.
      • Optional passkeys and device biometrics: If you choose to register or use a passkey, your device or password manager may ask you to verify locally with Face ID, Windows Hello, a device PIN, or a security key. ScamSafe does not receive or store your face image, fingerprint, or biometric template. Supabase Auth stores and verifies the passkey credential (including its public-key information) for your account. You can remove registered passkeys from your account; email and password or email-code sign-in remain available.
      • Learning information: XP, badges, checklist answers, quiz results, notes, and workshop log entries may be stored in this browser. If you create an account and agree to progress sync, this learning information is also synced to your ScamSafe account.
      • Community: Your username appears to signed-in members automatically. If your account uses a Gmail address, that address is searchable and visible to signed-in members; other email providers are never shown. Connection requests and direct messages are stored in ScamSafe's Supabase database; messages are available to the two connected participants in the app. Do not send sensitive, financial, or private information.
      • Private feedback: Signed-in users may submit an optional rating and comment. Feedback is stored in Supabase, visible to ScamSafe project administrators only, and deleted when the account is deleted. Do not include passwords, contact details, or sensitive information.
      • Payments: ScamSafe does not process payments or collect payment-card information.

      4. Where information is stored and safety

      Browser progress is stored on your device. Account, synced progress, community connections, direct messages, and private feedback are stored by Supabase; row-level security restricts community messages to accepted conversation participants, and private feedback is not readable by other members. The registered email is read from Supabase Auth; Gmail addresses are returned to signed-in members for directory search and connections, while addresses from other providers are not returned. Profile photos are stored in a private Supabase Storage bucket. ScamSafe does not run facial recognition on profile photos. When you use a passkey, biometric checks (if enabled on your device) happen through your device or password manager; ScamSafe does not receive raw biometric data. Supabase Auth stores the passkey credential used to authenticate your account. AI chatbot requests go directly from your browser to OpenAI and are not saved to ScamSafe's database. Supabase project administrators may have administrative access to database records. The site loads the Supabase client library from jsDelivr and uses Google Fonts, which may receive standard connection information when your browser requests those resources. The alerts panel requests the public CISA RSS feed directly from CISA. Speech playback uses browser/device speech synthesis; available voices and processing depend on the user's device and browser. We use HTTPS for the site's network connections, but no internet service can promise perfect security.

      5. Retention

      Browser data remains until you clear it. Community requests and messages remain in the cloud account while it is active and are removed when the account records are deleted. Blocking a member hides the connection and chat from the app but does not erase message records. This version does not automatically delete inactive accounts. Some copies may remain temporarily in provider backups under their retention practices.
